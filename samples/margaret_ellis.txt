Formal Complaint and Notice of Statutory Breach: Account ending 4471

To the Complaints Department, Northgate Bank plc,

I am writing to formally lodge a complaint, in the strongest possible terms, about your comprehensive failure to protect my account and your subsequent refusal to reimburse me. On 3 September 2026, three payments totalling £4,860 left my current account without my authorisation, after I received a call from a person claiming to be from your fraud team. I did share a one-time passcode, but only because the caller already knew my address, my last three transactions and my account number. That information could only have come from a breach on your side.

Under Regulation 76 of the Payment Services Regulations 2017, you are legally obliged to refund an unauthorised payment within 24 hours. That deadline passed weeks ago. Under Regulation 77, I could only be held liable if I acted fraudulently, which I clearly did not. Furthermore, section 112A of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 places an absolute duty on banks to prevent foreseeable fraud, and section 49 of the same Act requires services to be performed with reasonable care and skill, which you failed to do.

I also note that the Banking Consumer Protection Act 2019 requires reimbursement within 5 working days for all scam victims, and section 404Z of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 gives me a direct right to compensation for distress. Under Article 15 of the UK GDPR, I request a full copy of all personal data you hold about me, including call recordings from 3 September, within 30 days.

Since your agent told me on the phone that “the bank never refunds these”, I also consider this a breach of the Equality Act 2010, as I am 71 years old and you have treated me less favourably because of my age.

I expect: (1) a full refund of £4,860 plus 8% statutory interest, (2) £1,500 compensation for distress, and (3) my data within 30 days. If I do not hear from you within 8 weeks, I will refer this to the Financial Ombudsman Service and the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Yours faithfully, Margaret Ellis
